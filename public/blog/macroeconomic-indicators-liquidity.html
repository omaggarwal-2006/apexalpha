The Macroeconomic Environment as Market Driver

While microstructure and technical analysis govern short-term price movements, global macroeconomic factors dictate long-term market regimes and structural liquidity flows.

Understanding how central bank monetary policies, interest rate differentials, inflation metrics, and liquidity indicators interact allows traders to position portfolios in alignment with broad economic cycles.

1. The Global Liquidity Equation

Global financial market valuations are strongly correlated with the total supply of fiat currency and credit in the global banking system.

Net Central Bank Liquidity Formula: $$\\text{Net Liquidity} = \\text{Fed Balance Sheet} - \\text{Treasury General Account (TGA)} - \\text{Reverse Repo (RRP)}$$

  • Fed Balance Sheet Expansion (QE): Increases liquidity, inflating financial asset valuations.
  • TGA Rebalancing: When the US Treasury increases its general account balance at the Fed, it drains liquidity from commercial banks.
  • Reverse Repo Usage (RRP): When money market funds park cash at the Fed, it sterilizes liquidity out of active market circulation.
